Tool-using language agents are increasingly evaluated by final task success, but such scores conflate planning, tool selection, memory use, observation interpretation, recovery, and stopping behavior. We introduce a methodology for intervention-validity auditing and paired robustness inference, with \benchmark as its empirical vehicle. In the planned final study across [N] tasks, [M] intervention instances, and [K] agents, we will test whether clean success overestimates robust competence by [X] points on average, whether rank uncertainty changes under controlled interventions, and whether controlled robustness predicts naturalistic workflow outcomes. We also introduce Recovery-Aware Agent Control (\RAAC), a model-agnostic bounded controller that uses only observable interaction signals to verify, retry, cross-check, clarify, or abstain. The repository provides paired metrics, deterministic simulated tools, human-validation and leakage gates, resource-bounded execution infrastructure, and evidence-safe paper assets. Empirical results and \RAAC effects from completed non-oracle runs are not yet reported in this draft.
